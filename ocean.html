What are Ocean Currents?

Think of the ocean like a giant, moving highway. Instead of cars, it transports heat, nutrients, and organisms all around the planet.

There are two main types of "highways" in the ocean:

  • Surface Currents: Driven by wind and the Earth's rotation (top 10%).
  • Deep Currents: Driven by density—a mix of temperature and salt (bottom 90%).

Why do they matter?

Without these currents, the Equator would be way too hot for life, and the Poles would be completely frozen solid! The ocean acts like Earth's air conditioner.

Lab 2: Thermohaline Circulation

"Thermo" (Temperature) + "Haline" (Salt). Let's see how density creates a "Conveyor Belt."

Equator

Hot sun warms the water. Warm water is less dense.

Poles

Cold air chills water. Cold water is more dense.

Salt (Salinity)

Evaporation or sea ice leaves salt behind. Salty water sinks.

Freshwater

Melting glaciers add fresh water. It's less dense and floats.

DEEP OCEAN TANK

Observation: Watch how the "Cold" and "Salty" particles sink to the bottom. This is exactly what happens in the North Atlantic, starting the Global Conveyor Belt!
