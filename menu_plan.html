Learning Objectives

By the end of this lesson, participants will be able to:

  • Explain why menu planning supports health, budgeting, and time management.
  • Build a balanced one-day menu using food groups, portion awareness, and variety.
  • Adapt a menu for common dietary needs, preferences, and budget limits.
  • Evaluate a sample menu using a practical checklist.

Key Terms with Tooltips

Hover over or tab to each term to view a quick explanation.

Menu planningThe process of deciding meals and snacks in advance based on nutrition needs, schedule, budget, and preferences. helps reduce last-minute food choices.

Nutrient densityA measure of how many vitamins, minerals, fiber, or other beneficial nutrients a food provides compared with its calories. favors foods that give more nutrition per bite.

Portion sizeThe amount of food a person chooses to eat at one time. It may be larger or smaller than a recommended serving. affects total calorie and nutrient intake.

MacronutrientsNutrients needed in larger amounts: carbohydrates, protein, and fat. Each plays a different role in energy, repair, and body function. provide energy and structure.

Food securityReliable access to enough affordable, nutritious food for an active and healthy life. should be considered when planning realistic menus.

Meal prepPreparing ingredients or meals ahead of time to make healthy eating easier during busy days. saves time and reduces decision fatigue.

Mini Lesson: The 5-Part Menu Planning Method

1. Start with the person

Consider age, activity level, medical needs, culture, schedule, cooking skills, and budget.

2. Build around food groups

Plan meals with fruits, vegetables, grains, protein foods, and dairy or calcium-rich alternatives.

3. Add variety

Use different colors, textures, flavors, and preparation methods across the week.

4. Check practicality

Ask: Is it affordable? Available? Easy enough? Does it fit the person’s schedule?

5. Reflect and revise

Good menu planning is not perfection. It is a flexible plan that can be adjusted.

Instructor note: Emphasize that healthy planning should be realistic, culturally respectful, and budget-aware. Avoid framing food choices as “good” or “bad.” Instead, discuss balance, frequency, access, and context.
